## You never sleep
What if you never had to sleep? {#title}
Not once. Not ever.
The night you're born, your mom sings you a lullaby. {#lullaby}
You don't close your eyes.
She sings it again. And again. {#again}
By morning, she's asleep in the chair. You're still listening. {#plant}
Your body never needs rest. Your brain never switches off. {#rule}
Every night, while the world sleeps, you get eight extra hours.
That's the rule. {#thatsrule}
So what would you do with a third of a life that nobody else gets? {#restate}
## Childhood
Age five. {#age5}
Bedtime is the worst part of your day.
Your parents tuck you in at eight. You lie there until seven.
Eleven hours of ceiling. {#ceiling}
So you start sneaking into the kitchen. {#kitchen}
You read the cereal box. Then the cookbooks. Then the encyclopedia. {#read}
One night, your dad finds you there at four a.m. He makes you hot chocolate. Then he falls asleep at the table. {#dad}
Age ten. {#age10}
Your teacher asks how you finished the whole summer reading list in one weekend. {#school}
You tell her you don't sleep. She tells you to stop joking.
Nobody believes you. Not yet. {#notyet}

## Ten extra years
Age sixteen. {#age16}
You take the night shift at the twenty-four hour diner. {#diner}
The city goes dark, one window at a time. {#windows}
By three a.m., it's just you, the cook, and the streetlight. {#threeam}
By thirty, you've been awake eighty-seven thousand, six hundred hours longer than anyone you know. {#age30}
Ten extra years. {#tenyears}
You learn four languages. You read every classic. You get very, very good at chess. {#learn}
Then you do the math on a napkin. {#napkin}
Your mom is twenty-five years older than you. But she's slept through a third of her life.
At fifty, you'll have been awake exactly as long as she has. {#predict}

## Company
Age thirty-two. {#age32}
You meet Noor in the diner at four in the morning. She's a night nurse. {#meet}
For once, someone else is awake. {#company}
You get married at sunrise. {#married}
Then she switches to the day shift, and the nights are yours again. {#dayshift}
You have a son. You know every sound he makes in the dark. {#son}
Age fifty. {#age50}
You've now been awake exactly as long as your mother. {#crossing}
She falls asleep at your kitchen table, mid-sentence. You cover her with a blanket. {#blanket}

## The cost
But here's the part nobody sees. {#cost}
Every night, for eight hours, everyone you love disappears. {#disappear}
You're there for every fever and every bad dream. {#fever}
Your son grows up and moves away. When he can't sleep, he calls you at three a.m., because he knows you'll answer. {#calls}
Noor falls asleep on your shoulder for thirty years. {#shoulder}
You watch her grow old in the dark. {#grow}
Age seventy-five. {#age75}
The hospital is quiet at night. You know nights better than anyone. {#hospital}
Noor squeezes your hand. Go home, she says. Get some rest. {#rest}
After forty years, she still forgets. {#forgets}
She falls asleep. You stay awake. You always do. {#stay}
## The lullaby
Your granddaughter sleeps over, and she won't close her eyes. {#grand}
So you sing her the lullaby your mother sang to you. {#bookend}
She's asleep before the second verse. {#asleep}
And for the first time in eighty years, you wish you could follow her. {#follow}

## What's real
So, what's real? {#real}
Nobody can live without sleep. {#nobody}
Over New Year's 1964, a seventeen-year-old named Randy Gardner stayed awake for eleven days, for a science fair. By day four, he was seeing things. {#gardner}
Rats kept awake in a lab were dead or dying within a few weeks. {#rats}
But some people really are built for less. {#less}
In 2009, scientists found a mother and daughter with a tiny change in a gene called DEC2. They wake up rested after about six hours. {#dec2}
Dolphins sleep with one half of their brain at a time. Frigatebirds nap while they fly. {#animals}
So if you could have the extra twenty-six years, would you take them? {#ask}
Knowing everyone you love would be asleep for a third of them? {#ask2}
Tell me in the comments, and tell me which what-if to do next. {#cta}
